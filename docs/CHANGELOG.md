# NULLRUN — change log

History, not authority. Nothing here is binding: the build derives from
`NULLRUN-SPEC.md` alone, and by M1 only Part II and Part III of that document carry weight.

This file exists because the change log used to live inside the spec, where M5's word budget
applied to it. The budget is meant to force cuts to the *product*; instead it silently forced
cuts to the *record* — entries were compressed at every version and `0.1.0` through `0.2.1`
disappeared entirely after `0.4.0`. The text below was reconstructed from the `spec/*` tags,
taking the fullest surviving wording of each entry: 987 words, against the 251 still in the
spec when it was moved out.

**The division of labour.** A lesson learned in a version belongs in the spec — in M1–M5, an
invariant, a requirement, a §K check, or §O — where a rebuild will read it. The narrative of
how it was learned belongs here. If something here matters to the next build and is not also
in the spec, it will vanish, and that is correct.

Evidence for each version is in `conformance/<version>.md`. Spec and build states are tagged
`spec/<version>` and `build/<version>`.

- `0.9.3` Spec only, no build change. Added `tools/speclint.py`, which found three real defects on a spec declared clean one version earlier. **`NFR-8` had not existed since 0.4.0**: the FR renumber there ran `replace('FR-8','FR-9')` and `FR-8` is a substring of `NFR-8`, so the Presentation requirement was silently renamed — a corruption that survived five versions, four rebuilds and eighteen conformance checks because nothing verified that numbering was dense. `O` had a hole at 4 from the 0.9.0 merge. `I7` was guarded by no §K check, so "every screen has an exit" was an invariant nothing tested; `K8` now covers it. The linter checks duplicate ids, dense numbering, dangling references, invariants without checks, the M5 budget read from M5 itself, §T against the build's `CONFIG`, and that each version has a changelog and conformance entry — the last of which is the slip made in 0.9.1. A SQLite index was considered and rejected: these are constraint checks, not queries, and rows would destroy the one-pass readability M5 exists to protect.
- `0.9.2` Spec only, no build change. `K17` rewritten from *trace bites* to **trace binds**: a policy that ignores reach is ended by trace, and removing trace changes outcomes materially. The old check was measured against careful play, which finishes encounters in 3.8 of the 7 turns trace allows — so it could only pass by tuning the game until the clock killed players who chose correctly. Seven tunings were tried and none passed. Measurement shows trace is fully load-bearing: it ends **100%** of reach-ignoring runs, and removing it collapses careful play from 64% wins to 0% because reach becomes unbounded. Rewriting a failing check is how a checklist gets gamed, so the discrimination is recorded: alarm fired 0% against *both* careful and careless play and removing it changed nothing, while trace fires 0% / 100% / totally. The new check fails alarm and passes trace, which is the distinction it always meant to draw. Discharges the acceptance exception on `0.9.1`; every §K check now passes.
- `0.9.1` Offers state their **reach** — the most damage the current roster and breach could bring before trace ends the node, and whether that clears it (FR-10, `K18`). Found by playtest on seed `394LHH`: a run lost on layer 2 with firewall at 43 and never threatened, because ICE WALL at 89 integrity was impossible against a reach of 65 from the moment it was chosen. The build already knew — `damageBudget()` is used to enforce `I2` — and showed integrity, armour and payout, every number except the one that decided the outcome. A `gap`: the spec telegraphs intent values a full turn ahead (FR-3) but never applied the same honesty to picking a node, contradicting P3.1. The fix exposes a design problem it does not solve: 67% of offers are impossible, so the choice is usually two traps and one road — formerly invisible and lethal, now visible and thin. **Accepted by the author with `K17` outstanding**, overriding §K's rule that a rebuild passes only when every check does. Recorded rather than hidden: trace ends 0% of runs and the trace/clearability coupling is unresolved.
- `0.9.0` Change log moved out of the spec into this file. It is history, not authority — no rebuild reads it — and holding it inside a word-budgeted document meant M5 was cutting the record instead of the product. Entries had been compressed at every version and `0.1.0` to `0.2.1` had disappeared after `0.4.0`; all fourteen were reconstructed from the `spec/*` tags at their fullest surviving wording, 987 words against the 251 that remained. The spec drops 3,109 to 2,910 words, back under budget with no feature cut. `0.3.1` had already diagnosed this exact pressure and answered it by raising the budget, treating a structural problem as a number.
- `0.8.0` **Rebuild.** Restores two guarantees 0.5.0 destroyed with alarm. Payout had lost its only per-node term, so all three offers paid identically in 100% of 700 layers — a fake choice; payout now scales with the node's own integrity (FR-10). `I2` was deleted for mentioning alarm, taking the guarantee of a takeable node with it: offers draw three from four constructs, so a quarter of layers offered nothing clearable. `I2` returns as a function, enforced in generation. CORPSEC SWEEP, unclearable at every layer by any roster, cut to 95. K15 now requires the policy to enter every construct; it had entered two in 200 runs, which is why none of this was caught. M2 gains the rule this is the third instance of.
- `0.7.1` Art for nine contacts and five constructs (§C), embedded as data URIs. Recorded in §C because it would otherwise vanish at the next rebuild, as the free step and repair did. Build 33 KB to 92 KB of 150 KB.
- `0.7.0` **Rebuild.** Breach grows per layer (FR-5); firewall, repair and every construct's integrity and intents are retuned with it (§T, §C). Breach had to grow because integrity does: damage per node was capped at 57 while layer 6's softest node reached 58, capping the run at layer 5 — the 0.3.x bug's shape, one level up. Scaling breach alone passed K15 by collapsing encounters to one turn, so K15 now requires encounters of real length: a win bought by ending them instantly is not playability. The four quantities are over-constrained and were solved jointly.
- `0.6.0` **Rebuild.** Burn narrowed from run-scoped to node-scoped: the roster returns to ready on entering a node (I3, FR-4), and P3.3 narrows with it — spending is still losing, over one encounter rather than the whole run. Fixes the replenishment ratio that made 0.4.1 unwinnable: contacts burned about four per node while payout replenished one, so the roster was bankrupt by layer 2 regardless of firewall. A payout contact is now permanent growth rather than a patch. Measured on 0.4.1 as the largest available lever, 1.6 to 4.4 mean layers. Note the 0.3.1 run rejected this same change; that verdict was taken before breach existed and did not survive re-measurement.
- `0.5.0` **Rebuild.** Alarm cut entirely: the alarm level, its intent scaling, the carry-over, alarm nodes, HUNTER's alarm gate and invariant I2 all go. It was measured dead — alarm fired in 0% of runs in every configuration across builds 0.3.1, 0.4.0 and 0.4.1, because trace resets per node and encounters last two to three turns, so trace never reached 100. Trace takes its place as its own consequence: at 100 the run ends (FR-9). This keeps pillar 2 on far less machinery and gives breach a ceiling, since a free renewable attack could otherwise grind any construct down given unlimited turns. `K17` requires trace to actually end runs — a clock that never strikes is the mistake being cut.
- `0.4.1` Payout becomes a choice: contact or repair (FR-12), restoring the utility slot the 0.3.0 rebuild dropped from the old FR-11. I8 widened from damage to resources — the bug class was never specific to damage, and firewall had the identical structure: a run-long pool refilled only by contacts that burn. Measured on 0.4.0: 194 of 200 runs died on layer 3 at mean firewall 13.
- `0.4.0` **Rebuild.** Added breach (FR-5), a free renewable attack, and invariant I8 requiring one. This restores what the 0.2.x free step was silently doing: the old FR-6 documented only its I6 job, so the 0.3.0 rebuild faithfully preserved that job through bail and dropped the undocumented one — leaving per-encounter damage bounded by roster size and every encounter decided on entry. I8 and K16 exist so a rebuild cannot lose it again. FR-2 and I5 amended, later FR renumbered, `O4` resolved, `O12` and `O13` recorded. A `feature` classification changing invariants and the turn contract: a rebuild under M4.
- `0.3.3` Spec only. `O4` rewritten from the measurements: the blocker is structural, not a number, and the fallback `O4` itself proposed (per-encounter burn) is neither a `tune` nor sufficient — it changes I3, so M4 makes it a rebuild, and it does not make the game winnable. Recorded so the next rebuild does not retry it.
- `0.3.2` Spec only. Added `K15`, a viability check, after the first build passed all fourteen conformance checks while being unwinnable: §K tested that the rules hold, never that the game can be finished. `O4` upgraded from speculation to measurement.
- `0.3.1` Spec only, no rebuild; the first 0.3.x build is generated from this revision. M5 raised from 2,500 to 3,000 words: every requirement traces to the prototype, so the budget was cutting the record rather than the product. FR-3 now fixes an intent's value at display time, closing a `gap` where an alarm increment during end-of-turn resolution raised the resolved value above the value displayed all turn, violating I4 and pillar 1.
- `0.3.0` **Rebuild**, derived from the design prototype `nullrun.html`. Grid tactics cut entirely: movement, vision, positioning, map generation and their invariants go with FR-1. A visible roster of contacts that burn on use replaces the deck, so draw, discard, shuffle, hand, powers and scrub are cut, and card conservation becomes roster conservation (I3). Three node offers per layer replace the map (FR-9). Added persona and hardware (FR-13), roster cap and release (FR-12), armour (FR-6), portrait- and touch-first (NFR-5, FR-17), banded contact face (NFR-8). `O3` reversed. A `feature` classification touching S1, S2, S4, S6 and S7, past the M4 iterate threshold. Where the prototype disagreed with the draft spec beside it, the prototype won: FR-3, FR-7, FR-6, FR-11 and §T.
- `0.2.1` Spec only. `O3` resolved in favour of in-run deckbuilding.
- `0.2.0` **Rebuild.** Cooldown modules replaced by deck, hand, energy and discard; enemies act once per player turn; trace moved to per-turn.
- `0.1.0` Initial specification and first build.
