## Why K15 still fails: two further findings

### 1. Repair was dropped by the same rebuild that dropped the free step

`0.2.1 FR-11` offered "one utility (**repair**, implant, scrub, or data)" at every reward.
The 0.3.0 rebuild cut it. Firewall is a run-long pool whose only refill is healing
contacts — which burn. So survivability per run is bounded by roster size: exactly the
structure I8 was written to forbid for damage, still present for firewall.

Measured: 194 of 200 runs die on layer 3, entering nodes at mean firewall 13 with 1.8
unburned healers left.

Probe — a flat firewall restore at each cleared node, breach 5:

| repair | mean layers | win |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 2.0 | 0% |
| 10 | 2.4 | 0% |
| 14 | 2.8 | 0% |
| 18 | 3.4 | 0% |

Monotonic and insufficient alone.

### 2. Breach is flat; node integrity scales +14% per layer

The renewable source decays in relative terms as the run descends. Probe:

| config | win | mean layers |
|---|---|---|
| flat breach 5, repair 14 | 0% | 2.9 |
| flat breach 12, repair 14 | 3% | 4.7 |
| breach 5 +1/layer, repair 14 | 0% | 4.2 |
| breach 8 +1/layer, repair 20 | **18%** | 4.7 |

Only scaling breach *and* restoring firewall produces wins.

### 3. Alarm is structurally unreachable

Trace resets on entering a node (FR-9) and encounters last ~2.5 turns, so trace peaks near
25 of 100. Alarm fired in **0 of 200 runs** at every configuration tested. HUNTER, the alarm
intent scale, and the alarm carry-over are dead content: reachable only inside a single
20-turn encounter, which never happens. Either trace should persist across nodes, or alarm
needs a different trigger, or the whole system should be cut.

## Conclusion

Breach is correct and does its job; the design approved was sound but incomplete. Three
decisions remain, all `feature` or `gap` class, none of them mine to make: restore repair,
decide whether breach scales, and resolve what alarm is for.
