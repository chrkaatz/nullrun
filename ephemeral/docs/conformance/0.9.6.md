# Conformance run — build 0.9.6

Date: 2026-09-11 · Spec: 0.9.6 · presentation only, no rule change

Playtest feedback, from someone shown the build alongside the `traditional` arm: a screen of
dense figures and no idea what the game was. Both arms had it. Both stated every number
correctly and neither said what you were being asked to do.

## Classification

`gap` under M3, not `feature`. The build and the spec agreed with each other, and both were
wrong: no requirement said the game introduces itself, and so the build did not. A `gap` lands
in the relevant requirement rather than a new one, which is also what the M5 budget could
afford — see below.

`FR-17` amended: *"The loadout says in two sentences what the game is; the encounter restates a
turn in one line."* The encounter half is deliberate — a player can arrive there from a
screenshot or a handed-over phone without ever seeing the loadout.

## Iterate, not rebuild (M4)

Iterate holds on all three conditions:

- confined to one system in §S — `S7` presentation only
- no invariant in §I changes
- §K passes unchanged; no check was added, weakened or reworded

None of the six rebuild triggers fire: no §I change, no FR-2 change, one system, no untraceable
behaviour (the requirement was written before the build), and NFR-7 is not exceeded. This is
the first patch since the 0.9.5 art pass, so the drift budget stands at 1 of 3.

## M5 budget

The spec was at 2,988 words of 3,000 — twelve words of headroom against an amendment costing
eighteen. M5 says to cut the product, not the spec, but the cheaper honest cut was the change
log stub's meta-commentary, which is record rather than product and had already been moved to
`docs/CHANGELOG.md` in 0.9.0. Trimmed the stub by the same eighteen words.

**Spec is 2,988 words, exactly as before.** The requirement paid for itself.

## Note

The same gap existed in the `traditional` arm and was found by the same single piece of
feedback. Neither arm's conformance checks ask whether a first-time player can tell what the
game is — §K here tests that the rules hold and that the screen is legible, never that it is
comprehensible. Recorded rather than fixed: a check for it would have to assert on copy, and
what that check should say is not yet clear enough to write.
